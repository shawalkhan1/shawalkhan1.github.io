Human-Centered AI / Social Simulation

SYNQ

Exploring whether grounded digital twins and agent interactions can help people discover meaningful human connections.

The problem

Compatibility systems try to infer human relationships from limited signals. SYNQ explores a different design question: what might be learned from simulated interactions, and how can the people represented retain control?

My contribution

I founded SYNQ as an early-stage project. The proposed architecture combines a grounded personal representation, agent-to-agent interaction, and a compatibility layer. Consent, behavioral fidelity, and human oversight are central design concerns.

Evidence & current status

The available artifact is an interactive illustration of agent routines, conversations, and events. Use the controls to change the setting or pause the simulation. The venture remains at an early stage; no validated relationship outcomes are claimed here.

Limitations

Behavioral fidelity, consent, privacy, bias, and the risk of assigning people misleading scores require evaluation. The illustration does not establish that agents accurately represent individuals.

Next questions

How can users inspect, correct, and withdraw their representation? How should a system communicate uncertainty when simulated behavior informs a human decision?
