The problem

Access to an AI system does not guarantee that it is understandable, culturally relevant, or safe to use. Maternal healthcare makes those questions especially consequential: language, usability, fairness, and clinical correctness must be considered together.

My contribution

My current work evaluates and develops multilingual maternal-healthcare AI, including fairness, safety, linguistic disparities, and efficient deployment in low-connectivity environments. My engineering work spans speech, LLM, and retrieval pipelines through backend services.

This is collaborative work at the National AI Hub, LUMS, under Dr. Maryam Mustafa, with Aga Khan University as clinical partner.

How it works

Start with the interaction people can use.

Voice-first interaction addresses access barriers associated with reading and typing. The engineering question is how to support an understandable exchange in the languages and speaking styles of the intended users.

Evaluate what survives the language boundary.

Multilingual and code-mixed inputs can change how a model interprets a question. My work examines linguistic disparities alongside fairness and safety in maternal-healthcare systems.

Connect the model to relevant information.

Speech, LLM, and retrieval components form an end-to-end information pipeline. System design must account for both model behavior and the resources available to serve the interaction.

Evaluate usefulness alongside clinical risk.

The resulting interaction needs evaluation for clinical correctness, fairness, and helpfulness, as well as trust and usability. Field testing with rural users connects interface decisions to real constraints.

Conceptual workflow. Select a stage to explore its engineering and evaluation questions.

Evidence & current status

The system manuscript targets CHI 2027 and examines clinician-in-the-loop design and field testing with rural users, including trust calibration and over-reliance.

Limitations

Clinical performance and participant-level results are not available on this page. A voice interface is one part of accessibility. Reliable language handling, clinical evaluation, understandable responses, device sharing, and human oversight remain important. Efficiency must be evaluated alongside safety and usability.

Next questions

How can local or more resource-efficient inference preserve linguistic fidelity and clinically useful interaction when connectivity is limited? This connects my healthcare engineering to efficient AI and human-centered evaluation.
