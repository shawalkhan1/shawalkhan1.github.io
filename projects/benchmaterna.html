LLM Evaluation / Maternal Healthcare

BenchMaterna

A clinician-validated benchmark for evaluating language models on maternal healthcare in the Global South.

Shawal Latif, M. Mustafa, et al. · Collaborative research

Manuscript in preparation and submission / target ACL 2027

Problem My contribution Approach Evidence Limitations Next questions

The problem

Generic model evaluations do not establish that a system will provide clinically correct, fair, and useful maternal-health information across regional languages. The evaluation needs to reflect the context in which the system will be used.

My contribution

I co-built a clinician-validated evaluation of 30+ LLMs with 30 clinical experts, covering regional languages and six scoring dimensions. This work connects model benchmarking to the needs of maternal-healthcare users.

How it works

Evaluate models in the intended setting.

The benchmark focuses on maternal healthcare for women in the Global South and includes regional languages. Context shapes what a useful model response needs to accomplish.

Ground evaluation in expert judgment.

The benchmark is co-built with 30 clinical experts. Clinician validation provides a foundation for evaluating responses beyond general-purpose language-model scores.

Look beyond a single headline score.

The study scores models on six dimensions. Clinical correctness, fairness, and helpfulness are among the evaluation concerns described in my work.

Compare 30+ language models.

The benchmark evaluates 30+ LLMs. The purpose is to understand where model capability and contextual performance differ, rather than assume that a generic ranking transfers to maternal healthcare.

Study scope and evaluation design. Select a stage to explore the approach.

Evidence & current status

30+ LLMs evaluated
30 Clinical experts
6 Scoring dimensions

The manuscript targets ACL 2027. This page summarizes the study scope; it does not imply conference acceptance or release of the benchmark data.

Limitations

The public portfolio does not include the complete protocol, per-model scores, or a downloadable dataset. Those materials are needed to assess reproducibility and the boundaries of the evaluation.

Next questions

Which quality and fairness properties survive when a model is compressed or served on constrained hardware? That question links clinically grounded evaluation with efficient edge AI.
